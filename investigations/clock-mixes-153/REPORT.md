### 2.2 The 11 D-LAW misses

They are listed in `summary.md` ("D-LAW misses", generated). Every one has the same shape:

* **P-E2 / P-E3 / P-RA2 (9 misses).** The PHP side reads the caller clock at L531, and the Redis side reads `TIME` inside `BLOCK_GET`, a few milliseconds later. In each miss, a Redis second boundary fell between the twin's L531 reading and the end of its step (`boundary between: yes` on all 9). The registered predictor (§13 item 6) evaluates both sides at the L531 instant, so it is wrong exactly when the block's integer expiry falls in that gap.
* **P-RA1 (3 misses).** DV-B3's predictor uses the caller second of the L1780 reading. In two misses, the caller second advanced between that reading and the receipt of the response, from which the client's wait starts. In the third (E-DOCKER), the caller second advanced between the planned return and the L44 reading on return.

None of the 11 is a probe where the skew law of §1.2 failed while the clock readings and Redis commands sat in one second. The misses are a defect of the **predictor as registered**: it does not model the gap between a clock reading and the command it is compared with. This report does not redefine the detector. The registered result is FAIL, and this is its recorded cause.

**What it means for the numbers.** The in-window rates of §3 count the observed flips and decisions, not the predictions, so they are unaffected. The misses are 5 of 14,095 probes (0.04 %) in E-SAME and 6 of 1,767 (0.34 %) in E-DOCKER, where the gap is longer.

## 4. S-ATK: invalid, cause #155

The fixed-pace attacker (one pre-check every 30 s, phase per stream) failed D-AA in both policies, and every S-ATK cell contains exceptions. All of them are `malformed bounded metadata`, issue **#155**, in both twins, with no skew involved:

* 567 / 541 … 612 / 594 exceptions per cell (`S`, `Z`);
* 197–200 of the 200 streams of every cell hit it at least once.

The A/A first divergences coincide with a #155 exception at that step (39 of 42, 45 of 56) or earlier in the stream (the rest). An exception mid-request leaves the two twins' states different.

The cause is phase-locking: 30 s divides the 600 s and 900 s bounded windows, so a check falls in the last fraction of a second of a window it created 20 or 30 checks earlier (#155's mechanism). This is an observation for #155: a fixed 30 s cadence hits it in ≈ 99 % of streams within 40 min. It is not a measure of B–I.

The S-ATK flip and decision counts in `summary.md` are kept and are not reported as results here. A re-run with a pacing that is not a divisor of 600 / 900 s would change a registered condition, so it is a question for the Lead (§9).
